# What we have learned so far

After seven iterations the run has a firm picture of the collapse, a mechanism for why temperature does not undo it, a measured place where the choice surfaces in generation, internal handles that move the algorithm while keeping the programs, and no correct novel algorithm. Qwen3-8B [5] writes BFS and Dijkstra almost without exception on the two path search tasks and one algorithm on each of 13 further coding tasks (modal share 1.00 on 12, 0.98 on one). The collapse is not universal: three of 15 new tasks are already mixed in the instruct twin (trapped rain water, minimum spanning tree weight and valid anagram, whose minority shares in the twin's baseline samples range from 0.13 to 0.36), and on two of them the instruct model is more diverse than its base model. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct [22] attempts more algorithms but its correct grid programs are BFS 20 of 20; gemma-3-4b-it [76] writes DFS in about 70% and BFS in about 30% of programs for one grid prompt; Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct [124] shares most of Qwen3-8B's defaults.

The algorithm surfaces at a fork where it can be found. On the mixed units of Qwen3-8B and on Gemma's grid task, programs share an opening of 18 to 61 tokens and the algorithm is settled by the next token: ` Kr` or ` Prim` on minimum spanning tree weight, ` visited` or a blank line in Gemma; the entropy of the algorithm given the fork token's class is 0.05 to 0.19 bits. On connected components the fork is the token that names the routine (` def dfs` against ` def find`), and forcing it decides the algorithm in every slot. On kth smallest a ban decides before the first generated token, and on shortest path no single token decides within 60 tokens. The margin at the decision predicts what moves the algorithm. Under 40 random pushes, the fork margin on connected components ranks which pushes change the algorithm (Spearman 0.80), although a placebo that shuffles which fork tokens lead to which algorithm reaches the same value in 18% of permutations, so the ranking rests on how much probability stays on the default fork tokens. On kth smallest, where no fork exists, the log-likelihood ratio of a 20-token opening predicted the effect of 24 fresh pushes in a test committed before generation (Spearman 0.60).

Temperature acts on the algorithm only through the fork. Dividing the fork logits by T predicted the minority share at each temperature before sampling within the committed error on Gemma's grid task (mean absolute error 0.026) and on minimum spanning tree weight (0.083, with the observed drop twice the predicted one), just outside it on trapped rain water (0.105), and not in direction on valid anagram, where a second decision follows the fork. Lowering T from 1.0 to 0.3 collapses the output onto the default on minimum spanning tree weight and in Gemma (17:0 and 16:0 discordant slots), but not on trapped rain water (7:3) or valid anagram (2:5). Temperature above 1 raises the minority only as far as the fork logits allow: in Gemma from 0.34 to 0.46 at T 1.3, as predicted, and on the Qwen3-8B units by at most 0.06, where the predicted change is near zero. Top-k 10 gives the same program as top-p 1 because every fork token lies in the top 10. On the collapsed tasks the fork margins are 4.85 to 17.04 nats, so no temperature in range reaches the alternative: this is why the same-seed sweeps of iterations 1 to 4 changed the algorithm in 0 of 20 seeds on three tasks with thinking off and on the shortest path task with thinking on, while phrasings multiplied. The answer to question 1a-c of the request is that a model has few ideas at tight temperature and the same few at loose temperature, in proportions its fork logits fix. On other problems, temperature above 1 has been reported to raise algorithmic diversity at a correctness cost [72]; the fork margin says where that can happen.

A random displacement of the task-specification states at early to middle blocks changes the algorithm in Qwen3-8B, and its size has to be counted in the right unit. At the absolute unit of iteration 4, which scales the noise of each cell to the displacement a ban causes there, bf16 escapes on 26 of 40 directions on connected components and 6 of 40 on shortest path, close to the 29 and 8 of the 8-bit engine on which the effect was found, with the same directions escaping. Iteration 5's weak plan noise came from its ban-free unit, which scales the noise to a fixed fraction of the residual norm, not from its seeds (unit effect +0.45 [0.35, 0.55], seed effect +0.03, on connected components; replicated on fresh slots and on kth smallest): the absolute unit concentrates 81% of its energy on a few task tokens, and the same energy at shuffled positions moves almost nothing. A default-to-runner-up axis, the second choice being the algorithm a model ranks second, is not the channel these pushes use (Spearman 0.17 and 0.01; clamping it removes nothing), although pushing along it at four times the distance between the forced-prompt centroids, beyond the committed doses, moves all 30 connected components slots off the default (29 to DFS, 1 to BFS) with every program passing. Priced as a knob at the absolute unit, plan noise beats an intent sentence written by the base model on kth smallest and the grid and loses on connected components, shortest path and two sum; enumerate and pick and generate then select [43] reach more raw diversity at large pass costs.

Where the noise must enter is not settled. In bf16 at the ban-free unit, noise at the task-specification positions moves the algorithm while the read position and the condition span barely do. The preregistered site rule was nonetheless withdrawn, for lack of common support and for breakage at alpha 8. A post-hoc model at alpha 4 or less puts the read position below the task-specification site on both tasks but not the condition span, so the site of the choice remains open; on connected components noise acts most at blocks 0 to 11 while the exchange transfers at blocks 12 to 23. Noise in generated tokens 1 to 32 also redirects the choice, and against a temperature matched on per-token KL it was not established (12:6 and 7:3 discordant flips). Under bf16 these internal results hold: the route of a ban (iterations 2 and 3), the band patching on kth smallest and connected components, the Stage A exchange, the escape fractions and per-sample plan-noise rates of iteration 4 at their absolute unit, the transplant results of iteration 5 and the read-position transplant that breaks the output without moving the algorithm. Every experiment of iteration 7 ran in bf16. The 8-bit site odds ratio (5.72), the same-seed contrast of plan noise (17.25 of 20 seeds) and the forced-prompt subspace tests are 8-bit results that were not rerun.

Internal handles now exist in two families, each with its own model, site and controls (Table 279 and Section 7.9). In Qwen3-8B, exchanging the task-specification states of a ban or a forced instruction into a neutral twin transfers the algorithm (shortest path 21 of 30 correct programs, kth smallest 16 of 30; forced instructions 24 to 30 of 30), and a DAS subspace of rank 1 or 2 at blocks 12 to 23 matches that exchange in generation while random subspaces of the same rank do nothing. That subspace is a steering handle and not a certified variable: permuted labels transfer 0.47 to 0.70 of its effect, an untrained network admits a fit 0.92 as good [132], and the reverse interchange does not restore the default [122]. The effect of scaling it is a ramp, not a step. In Gemma, DAS at the fork passes the random-subspace, permuted-label and untrained-network controls, but the subspace is not unique across seeds and the committed rule does not license calling it one-dimensional; as a dose dial it raises correct BFS programs from 11 to 42 of 50, above the 35 of an explicit BFS instruction, while a difference-in-means edit toward BFS at matched teacher-forced effect destroys the programs. A path-patching circuit for Gemma's fork margin, validated against matched random components in the real model, runs from MLPs at the condition sentence to a stack of MLPs and one head at the fork position; steering its top five nodes doubles correct BFS programs. A natural language autoencoder on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct names the algorithm at the token where the prompt names it and not later, and an outside reader of the visible text predicts the algorithm better, also with one unit per sample (0:25). The read position is inert as a carrier in Qwen3-8B and in the NLA's reading.

A single prompt ban, where the model obeys it, removes the default and the output collapses on one replacement: Bellman-Ford/SPFA on the shortest path task, DFS on the grid task, and quickselect and size-k heap, brute force, index marking and BFS on the bench tasks where Qwen3-8B obeys. Shown a correct program of another algorithm and told not to use it, the model goes home to its default. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct mostly ignores bans. How often a ban is obeyed depends on its wording more than on anything else measured, and the fourth template, which states only that the algorithm is not allowed here, is the weakest in every artifact and model tested. Bans of five names fail by reimplementation in both families tested, and a push of the grid task's states toward the survivors of a top-five ban, inside the forced-prompt manifold, produced BFS in all 300 programs. In the fitted coordinates of shortest path, bans sit outside a paraphrase null on the runner-up side, while random seeds and long novelty instructions sit inside it and leave the algorithm where it was; the geometry does not predict where a ban lands.

Where a ban acts depends on the task. On the path tasks it is carried mostly by the prompt states (likelihood readout 0.85; behaviour 0.98 on the shortest path task, mixed on the grid task); on the bench, pooled over five gated tasks, the direct route carries 0.744 [0.38, 1.11] of the behavioural effect and the prompt states 0.611 [0.06, 1.17], with overlapping intervals, and two sum, classed as direct, also carries 0.43 of its ban in the task-specification states. The ban is enforced at the opening of the program body, to a single token on kth smallest, the same place where forks surface. The read position predicts how strongly a wording will be obeyed (-0.41 across 110 wordings) but carries none of the effect when exchanged or spliced, and push movability as a forecast of which bans a model obeys is closed. What decides the replacement has measured leads and no confirmed rule. The base model's runner-up names the push landing in 17 of 21 recorded events and beats the instruct twin's own ranking of algorithm openings 8:1 where the two disagree (p 0.020, retrospective), but where the twin already writes an alternative that alternative names the landing as often (6 of 7), and on the 8 held-out events where it writes none the base runner-up named 5 (p 0.053). The base model's variety does not say whether a task can be moved (Spearman 0.15 over 13 new units, measured at the ban-free unit; at the absolute unit kth smallest and connected components are movable and shortest path, two sum and the grid are not), and writing its task-specification states into the instruct prefill reopened its runner-up on 1 of 5 Qwen3-8B tasks and 2 of 7 Gemma tasks.

No instruction, knob, push, transplant, feature clamp, verbal edit or fitted edit produced a correct novel algorithm, and neither did evolutionary search. Iterations 1 to 3 produced 116 correct programs outside the catalogue and iteration 5 another 130, and none was new; iteration 4 found none among 14,206 correct programs, and the second blind adjudication of iteration 6, with controls of known class, overturned the only novel call on a full read. In iteration 7 the run tried the documented route to new programs [81, 82], an evolutionary search over a MAP-Elites archive [133] with an executable evaluator and a novelty critic, on three tasks. On kth smallest it found 78 distinct correct programs that implement known techniques outside the catalogue (Fenwick-tree order statistics, binary search on the value range, bucket select, partial selection sort, an order-statistic treap), against 0 for enumerate and pick, 0 for the same prompt without evolution and 2 for the instruction alone. Counting every arm, including a larger mutator, 103 distinct correct programs implement these techniques, and none is novel. On shortest path and trapped rain water the search found only catalogue variants. The judge gate for novelty failed for every judge family tried, so these classes rest on manual and rule-based reads.

What remains open follows from this. First, whether Qwen3-8B's handle can be certified as a variable: a DAS fit at the fork in generation, where Gemma's controls passed, rather than at the task-specification states. Second, where shortest path decides, since its opening likelihood predicts pushes better the later it is measured. Third, a temperature prediction that composes more than one fork, for the two mixed units where one fork did not suffice. Fourth, the transcoder attribution graph at Gemma's fork, which failed on a 16 GB GPU. Fifth, evolutionary search on a task whose catalogue already covers the known techniques, so that a correct program outside it would be new, the remaining route to a novel algorithm. Of the items of the request, these have been run. Geometry plots in activation coordinates exist at a label-dependent position (iteration 2), at the plan site (iteration 4), for the top-five prohibition and the fork (iteration 6) and in the fitted DAS coordinates (iteration 7). The top-five prohibition was applied by prompt, token veto, plan push and in-manifold push. Natural BFS against DFS internals were read within a ban in Qwen3-8B and within one prompt in Gemma, where a circuit for the fork was validated. Thought anchors and forks, the logit and tuned lenses, the same-seed test with thinking on and off and with top-k 10 (now explained by fork margins), natural language autoencoders on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, sparse autoencoders (Qwen-Scope on Qwen3-8B, Gemma Scope 2 on Gemma), DAS with the full set of published controls and evolutionary search with an evaluator were all run.
