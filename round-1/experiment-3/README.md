## Layman Summary

Tests whether a code-writing AI model settles on its algorithm within its first few words, by restarting its answers from many cut points and reading its internal states.

## Full Summary

Screen of 'fork tokens decide the idea' on Qwen3-8B (bf16, thinking off, vLLM 0.11.0) with two path-search coding tasks built here (T1 unweighted grid path, T2 weighted shortest path; 40 seeded hidden tests each, 17 reference solutions) under a neutral baseline prompt and a modal-ban prompt (ban:BFS on T1, ban:Dijkstra on T2). Labeller: sandboxed runtime tracer (instrumented heapq/deque/queue, grid reads, edge passes, call depth) + AST rules + OpenRouter judge on disputes (gold-set gate). Measurements on one shared pool of ~10k labelled generations: (1) S6 pools at global T in {0.3,0.7,1.0,1.3}; (2) Forking-Paths-style resampling screen over 40 base paths (S=8 every 4th token over the first 92 tokens for ban paths), permutation-calibrated multinomial change points, token-level localisation, S=30 confirmation with control pairs and next-token entropies; (3) prompt swap at the commitment token; (4) fork-window temperature knob vs global-T sweep, matched random-window and AdapT-style anchored controls; (5) residual-stream probes (layers 8-28) at R, k=8/16/32/64, fork and commitment positions vs prompt-only predictor with permutation nulls. Outputs: generations/*.jsonl, forks.json, swap.json, knob_table.json, probes.json, summaries/, figures/, states/ (split npz parts), method_out.json (exp_gen_sol_out) with the pre-registered verdict and rule trace.
