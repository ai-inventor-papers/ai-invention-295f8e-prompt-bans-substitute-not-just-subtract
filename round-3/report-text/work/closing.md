# What we have learned so far

After three iterations the run has a firm picture of the collapse, a measured picture of where and how a prohibition acts, and still no rule for what replaces the default when the default itself is banned. Qwen3-8B [5] writes BFS and Dijkstra almost without exception on the two path search tasks and one algorithm on each of 13 further coding tasks (modal share 1.00 on 12, 0.98 on one). Sampling temperature, top-k, top-p, thinking mode and random seeds change how the code is worded and not which algorithm it implements; with seeds coupled across temperatures the algorithm changed in at most 1 of 30 seeds per cell. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct [22] attempts more algorithms, but its correct grid programs are BFS 20 of 20; counted over correct programs it is more diverse than Qwen3-8B on 9 of 13 bench tasks.

A single prompt ban, where the model obeys it, removes the default and the output collapses again on one replacement. On the path tasks the replacement is Bellman-Ford/SPFA (272 of 272 compliant iteration-1 programs, 255 of 260 iteration-2 attempts not banned) and DFS (116 of 128; 257 of 257). On the bench, Qwen3-8B obeys the modal ban on only 5 of 12 tasks, and where it does the landing replicates across three engines and is specific to the task: quickselect and size-k heap, brute force, index marking, BFS, and a failing merge without sorting. Shown a correct program of another algorithm and told not to use it, the model goes home: the default's share of attempts rises from 0.05 under the copy prompt to 0.72 under the ban. How often a ban is obeyed depends on its wording more than on anything else measured; the template `X is not allowed here` is the weakest in every artifact, and dropping it alone changes the Llama verdict.

What decides the replacement under a modal ban is not settled, and the reasons are now specific. The likelihood instrument built in iteration 2 cannot decide it: its gains never point at the algorithm the model writes (0 of 17 events), and a decision on estimable mass would need about 50 programs per algorithm and 129 Qwen3-8B events. On sampled events, substitution by association is not supported in Qwen3-8B. Association was worse than uniform on all five gated modal bench bans and lost to the context-free default on exposure bans by about 1.3 nats per attempt; in Llama it ties the ban-free forecasts, and the landing is predictable above chance (7 of 13 hits for both). The ban-free forecasts fare no better on the modal bans: no family beats uniform on average, three of five landings were named by no family, and the signature vetoes meant to read the next rung in advance almost never remove the default, which comes back renamed or respelled. On the shortest path task every perturbation that moves the model off Dijkstra, random activation directions included, lands on the same second choice as the ban, so a shared top rung exists there; the order below it is unmeasured. The model's free-form answer predicts the path task substitutes, which may reflect simulation of its own behaviour [39]. Naming an algorithm inside a prohibition primes it, in both models, most starkly when a list that leaves only the default produced quickselect 50 of 50 times.

Where a ban acts depends on the task. On the path tasks it is carried mostly by the prompt states (likelihood readout 0.85; behaviour 0.98 on the shortest path task, mixed 0.50 and 0.35 on the grid task). On the bench, behaviourally, generated tokens reading the ban carry more of the pooled effect than the prompt states (0.74 against 0.61), with a redundant mechanism on two tasks, a direct one on two and a superadditive one on one, all reproduced by a second draw. What is common is the point of enforcement: forcing the first content token of the default's body undoes the ban completely, while forcing the `def` line undoes none of it, much as safety refusals are enforced in the first tokens [63]. The read position predicts how strongly a wording will be obeyed (-0.41 across 110 wordings) but splicing it alone carries none of the effect, a shared prohibition direction written at the task specification positions is no better than a random push of the same norm, ablating the three heads that attend to the banned name does no more than ablating random heads, and no within-ban locus exists at generated tokens 1 to 16, where the template sets the class. Every activation edit the run tried either did nothing, broke the model, or acted like a random direction; decodable is not the same as actionable here [55].

No knob opens the repertoire cheaply. No knob reaches an even two-algorithm split under 20 points of pass rate; the semantic seed (Bellman-Ford/SPFA 61, Dijkstra 30 at 7 points) and the A* mention are the two conditions that buy a second correct algorithm, and both name or describe it. Generate then select, which hands the choice to an external random draw [43], reaches three or four algorithms at 24 to 42 points. Ban lists fail by reimplementing the banned mechanisms, disguised in 41% to 92% of cases; cumulative token vetoes are exact but stall at the first or second rung on all six tasks tried; and bans that leave one survivor reach interchangeable alternatives (BFS for connected components) but not algorithms the model can write on request such as Floyd-Warshall. No instruction or knob produced a correct novel algorithm: 0 of 78 correct programs outside the catalogue in the first two iterations and 0 of 38 in the third are novel, and the request's long-form instruction makes 84% of outputs claim novelty while their code is catalogue algorithms or nothing.

Post training sharpened an ordering the base model shows under the chat template (preregistered verdicts: INSTALLED_BY_POST_TRAINING on the shortest path task, MIXED on the grid task, MIXED on both at the planned sample size), consistent with reports that post training narrows output diversity [57, 35]; in plain completion the base model's shortest path mode is Bellman-Ford/SPFA.

What remains open follows from this. The replacement rule under a modal ban needs more gated events where the families disagree than five per model, with the template modelled as a random effect; Llama's present minor events, where the named ban sometimes gained the associate, are the most informative place left. The first body token is a behavioural locus that no internal readout has yet examined. The request items not delivered are a same seed temperature arm with thinking on and an SAE readout that keeps the ban's displacement.
