# Iteration 3: ban-free forecasts on the bench, the route of a ban across twelve tasks, and how far any knob reaches

## 3.1 Why this iteration ran what it ran

**The review of the iteration-2 record.** The review raised nine major and three minor items. Five concerned evidence the record held but read too strongly. The likelihood substitution test could not settle where a ban sends the mass: on the only rows with known behaviour, its algorithm specific residual pointed away from the substitute the model actually wrote, and its noise correction was never checked on Qwen3-8B. The record also carried two verdicts on the rival account that contradicted each other (a shared second choice on the shortest path task, and "no coherent ladder" among the things ruled out). The route claim was stated in the closing summary as "the direct route none", although two of the three readouts in the same artifact showed a direct route. The cross-model contrast rested on failing programs: Llama's correct grid programs were all BFS. The knob verdict was paraphrased past its own metric. Four items concerned work that existed but was missing from the record: the ban list experiment's 13 manifold figures with centroids coloured by implemented count, the out-of-catalogue programs that no one had inspected, positioning rows for the iteration-2 claims, and a statement that the forecast contest of the four frozen families had never been run. The remaining items were the preregistered predictions and their outcomes, the inherited bench instrument, and five factual slips. The corrections are made in place in Sections 1.12 and 2.3 to 2.12, each marked; Table 80 maps every item to where it is answered.

**The hypothesis update.** The update flipped the predicted branch to the side the evidence leaned toward and moved the decision from likelihood cells to sampled programs. Its title claim is that a prompt prohibition removes the named default and does not choose the replacement: the mass that leaves the default lands on what we call the *next rung*, the survivor that the model's own distribution puts next once the default is gone. The rival is substitution specific to X, under which the landing follows the model's stated alternative to X (association) or the survivor whose programs look most like X's (proximity). Under total collapse the next rung cannot be read from sampled frequencies, so the update introduced ban-free counterfactuals that expose it without any ban text.

- We call the *signature veto* a decoding-time mask on the default's distinctive token n-gram: the shortest n-gram (up to 6 tokens) present in at least 80% of the default's baseline programs that minimises its largest share in the other algorithms' forced programs. The one-shot form masks the completing token the first time it comes up; the persistent form masks it at every occurrence; the iterated form adds the n-grams of re-emerged default programs for up to three rounds. The *subtraction forecast* of an event is the landing of the last veto round that leaves at least 20 attempts off the default.
- The *ladder forecast* pools the landings of generic pushes that move at least 20 attempts off the default: the nonce ban, the novelty instruction and three random directions added at the task specification positions at the ban's own displacement norm.
- The *association forecast* is the model's frozen free-form answer to "what is the standard alternative to X", and the *proximity forecast* is the survivor whose forced programs are nearest to the default's by tree edit distance on identifier-normalised syntax trees [64].
- We call an *exposure ban* a prompt that shows a correct program of a non-default algorithm Y and then asks for a correct solution that does not use Y. Showing Y makes the model copy it, so Y becomes the default to ban, and association and ladder disagree wherever Y's stated alternative is not the model's context-free default.

The update also added a test of the shared ban direction at the positions that carry the ban causally, a locus probe at generated tokens 1 to 8, the request's top-five prohibition asked again as a cumulative token veto, and the request's own long-form novelty instruction. The event gate stayed as in Section 2.2 (avoidance at least 0.5 and at least 20 attempts not banned). The likelihood instrument was demoted to a descriptive forecast of mass. The two path search tasks became the screen, and the 12 frozen bench tasks in two models became the confirmation; the two are never pooled.

**The iteration-2 preregistered predictions and their outcomes.** These were not listed in the iteration-2 record; they are recorded here as the hypothesis update recorded them.

1. A gain for DFS under the BFS ban specific to X, larger than the ladder can match: failed. The largest residual of that row belongs to IDDFS (+7.99) and DFS's is +0.83.
2. The direct route at generation time carries at least two thirds of the ban: failed. The direct share was 0.00 in likelihood, 0.35 in grid behaviour and 0.05 in shortest path behaviour; the preregistered alternative, decided before writing, was met on the likelihood readout.
3. A compliance correlation at the read position of -0.5 or below: missed (-0.41 [-0.69, -0.17], p = 0.0003).
4. The validity gate of the likelihood instrument: failed in both models in its preregistered, name scrubbed form.
5. The locus rule: no position passed on the grid task, and the shortest path pass was a readout after commitment.
6. The positive knob target: not met.
7. The forecast contest of the four frozen families (ladder, association, prime and veto, proximity) under the preregistered rule: not run on any new event. Only the three gated iteration-1 events existed, with a largest margin of 0.05 nats, and the ban list landing forecasts were degenerate.

**The route, restated per task and per readout.** The closing summary of iteration 2 said the direct route carried none of the ban. Per readout, the record holds this: on the shortest path task the prompt route carries the ban in likelihood (0.82) and in behaviour (0.98 [0.89, 1.07], direct 0.05 [0.01, 0.09]); on the grid task the likelihood readout is dominated by the opening fork (0.87), behaviour is mixed (prompt 0.50 [0.39, 0.62], direct 0.35 [0.25, 0.45]) and the natural proposal readout puts the direct route at 0.16 [0.11, 0.21]; Llama has the likelihood readout only (0.84 [0.62, 1.46]). Splicing the read position alone carries none of the effect (F_R about 0), although the read position projection correlates with compliance across wordings. Decodable there is not actionable there [55].

**The strategy.** Seven artifacts ran (Table 79). One audit without generation repaired the record. Three experiments sampled the bench bans that decide the title claim, each committing its forecasts by sha256 hash before its own first ban sample, because artifacts of one iteration run in parallel and cannot share commitments: modal bans in Qwen3-8B [5], exposure bans in Qwen3-8B, and modal and present minor bans in Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct [22]. Three experiments asked where and how far: the behavioural route on the bench, the internal locus with the shared prohibition direction, and the reach of prompt bans and token vetoes with the novelty question. The budget went to sampled events rather than likelihood variants, as the review asked.
