# What we have learned so far

After five iterations the run has a firm picture of the collapse, a complete answer on temperature, a prefill site at which noise is effective (not a locus of the decision, and no representation of the choice), a measured but incomplete account of what a prohibition does, and a split verdict on where the alternatives come from. Qwen3-8B [5] writes BFS and Dijkstra almost without exception on the two path search tasks and one algorithm on each of 13 further coding tasks (modal share 1.00 on 12, 0.98 on one). Sampling temperature, top-k, top-p, min-p, thinking mode and random context seeds change how the code is worded, not which algorithm it implements. With the token seed coupled across temperatures from 0.3 to 1.3, the algorithm changed in 0 of 20 seeds on three tasks with thinking off. It also changed in 0 of 20 on the shortest path task with thinking on, under top-p 1 and under top-k 10. Meanwhile the number of phrasings per idea rose from 3 to 13 on the grid task. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct [22] attempts more algorithms but its correct grid programs are BFS 20 of 20, and gemma-3-4b-it [76] shows the same temperature null (best Hill-1 within 5 pass points at most 1.32). On other problems temperature above 1 has been reported to raise algorithmic diversity at a correctness cost [72], so the null belongs to these tasks.

The algorithm can be influenced, and where it can depends on the task, and on the engine and dose unit at which it is measured. Under the 8-bit engine of iteration 4, a random displacement of the task-specification states at early to middle layers, sized to the displacement a ban itself causes, changes the algorithm the model writes. Single random directions at that size changed it in 3 to 17 of 20 coupled seeds on the shortest path task, against 0 for temperature (17.25 of 20 for the union over subsets of three directions). The same displacement at the read position or on the condition sentence changes nothing at per-position doses up to 4 ban units (0 of 526 and 0 of 527 correct programs off the default). At matched energy it is far weaker but not inert (+0.23 on find duplicate, +0.18 on connected components). The late layers are almost inert, and the advantage of the task-specification site survives matching on downstream displacement (odds ratio 5.72 [3.94, 8.30]). Noise in generated tokens 1 to 32 also redirects the choice, more than the plan site on find duplicate, so the choice is still open after prefill. Under the bf16 engine and the ban-free dose unit of iteration 5, however, only kth smallest and connected components escape with fixed random directions, and per-sample plan noise moves more than 10% of programs off the default only on kth smallest; the cause of this shrinkage is not established.

Priced as a knob, plan noise is not the practical answer. Under the 8-bit engine it was the cheapest route to a second correct algorithm on kth smallest (Hill-1 2.72 within 5 pass points) and connected components (2.09). Fixed random directions reached above 2 at no cost on kth smallest and two sum, but these are maxima: the best of 21 direction cells on kth smallest (7 qualify) and of 9 directions on two sum (1 qualifies), where fresh per-sample noise reaches only 1.29. Against the text-level plan samplers that the iteration-4 comparison lacked, plan noise matched the best sampler on 1 of 5 decisive tasks. An intent sentence written by the base model, with the code written by the instruct model, won on connected components, the shortest path task and two sum (2.53, 1.45 and 1.61 against 1.09, 1.18 and 1.17). Enumerate-and-pick reached the most raw diversity, at 16 to 62 pass points. Plan noise is not decoding in disguise: at alpha 2 it moved 19 slots against 3 for decoding matched to its per-token departure from the twin. On merge intervals, topological order and nonoverlapping intervals no non-exposure knob reaches 1.5 within 20 pass points. Generate then select [43] and enumerate-and-pick, which hand the choice to an external random draw, remain ceilings with large pass costs.

Where pushes move the model, the base model says where they will land. Given the same chat-templated prompt, the untuned base model spreads over more algorithms than the instruct model on most tasks (base-diverse on 5 of the 15 existing tasks and intermediate on 7). Its most frequent alternative is where the instruct model goes when nudged. This held on 5 of 5 movable existing tasks, on 6 of 7 events on new tasks (Poisson-binomial p 0.003) and on 5 of 7 in Gemma (p 0.025), and in panel A no nudged program landed outside the base repertoire. A forecast built from base samples, before any push, beats the model's own stated alternative on log score (by 0.75 nats on new tasks and 1.50 in Gemma). The base model does not, however, say whether a task can be nudged at all: on unseen tasks the base off-default share ranks movability at Spearman 0.15 (13 units), base-diverse tasks such as count primes and valid anagram (0.81 each) never moved, and simpler rivals (the runtime merit of the fastest alternative, the twin's own modal share) tracked movability better. Writing the base model's task-specification states into the instruct prefill reopened the base runner-up on 1 of 5 base-diverse Qwen3-8B tasks and 2 of 7 Gemma tasks. In Gemma both successes were tasks whose instruct twin already wrote the runner-up 8% to 12% of the time. On kth smallest a random displacement of the same norm moved more programs than the base states. Post-training narrowed what the base model writes, consistent with reports that it narrows output diversity [57, 35, 87], and the narrowed alternatives are where nudges land; but the narrowing is not carried by the task-specification states of blocks 12 to 23 alone.

Where the choice is represented is not established. The task-specification states are a site at which noise is effective; a subspace spanned by the centroids of forced-algorithm prompts there is reliable (split-half cosine 0.89 to 0.97) yet does not predict which random directions move the algorithm (within-task Spearman 0.03), and removing the component of a push in it leaves the effect intact. Which directions move the algorithm is reproducible across templates on four tasks (0.76 to 0.90) and less so on the grid task (0.39) and find duplicate (0.59), and signed progress toward the runner-up, the one exploratory predictor, concentrated on connected components and was closed.

A single prompt ban, where the model obeys it, removes the default and the output collapses on one replacement. On the path tasks the replacement is Bellman-Ford/SPFA (272 of 272 compliant iteration-1 programs, 255 of 260 iteration-2 attempts not banned) and DFS (116 of 128; 257 of 257), and on the shortest path task every perturbation that leaves Dijkstra, random activation directions included, lands on Bellman-Ford/SPFA, which is also the base model's runner-up there. On the bench Qwen3-8B obeys the modal ban on 5 of 12 tasks, and on 4 once avoidance counts only catalogue algorithms, because merge intervals passes the gate only through failing programs labelled other. Where it obeys, the landing replicates across three engines: quickselect and size-k heap, brute force, index marking, BFS. Shown a correct program of another algorithm and told not to use it, the model goes home to its default (0.05 of attempts under the copy prompt, 0.72 under the ban), and Llama does the same on five of six present minor bans. How often a ban is obeyed depends on its wording more than on anything else measured; the fourth template, which states only that X is not allowed here, is the weakest in every artifact and in Gemma.

What decides the replacement under a modal ban has a measured lead and no confirmed rule. The likelihood instrument of iteration 2 cannot decide it (its gains never point at the algorithm the model writes, 0 of 11 distinct rows). Substitution by the stated alternative of the model is not supported in Qwen3-8B, where association was worse than uniform on all five gated bench bans, and in Llama it is the only family above chance once programs labelled other are excluded (7 of 13, p = 0.032). The landing of random pushes forecasts the seen Qwen3-8B bench bans best on matched events: -0.76 against -3.11 for association on the 3 events where the push forecast is defined, and -1.37 against -3.46 over all 5 with an undefined forecast scored as uniform. It hits 2 of 3 defined events and misses connected components, where the ban lands on BFS and pushes on DFS. On connected components either route of the ban alone lands mostly on DFS and both routes together choose BFS, but this is a single-task observation: on find duplicate the direct route alone lands on index marking, neither the push landing nor the association answer, and the landing there depends on the site at which noise enters. Naming an algorithm inside a prohibition primes it in every family tested, most starkly when a list that left only the default produced quickselect 50 of 50 times and when Gemma, told not to write a hand-made binary search, wrote one 99% of the time.

Where a ban acts depends on the task. On the path tasks it is carried mostly by the prompt states (likelihood readout 0.85; behaviour 0.98 on the shortest path task, mixed 0.50 and 0.35 on the grid task). On the bench, pooled over five gated tasks, the direct route carries 0.744 [0.38, 1.11] of the behavioural effect and the prompt states 0.611 [0.06, 1.17], overlapping intervals with a negative interaction; without merge intervals the fractions are 0.865 and 0.648. The ban is enforced at the opening of the body, to a single token on kth smallest and 10 to 30 tokens elsewhere, much as safety refusals are enforced in the first tokens [63]. The read position predicts how strongly a wording will be obeyed (-0.41 across 110 wordings) but splicing it alone carries none of the effect, a shared prohibition direction is no better than a random push of the same norm, and ablating the heads that attend to the banned name does no more than ablating random heads.

Push movability as a forecast of which bans a model obeys is closed. On the Qwen3-8B screen every movable task obeyed and no immovable one did, and the escape fraction ranks ban avoidance at Spearman 0.86 across 14 tasks. In the held-out family, however, the preregistered test was inconclusive under the primary rule and disconfirmed under the default-drop sensitivity (balanced accuracy 0.57, Fisher p 1.0, Spearman 0.39). The positive rank correlation rests on a tied-baseline task and a mislabelled task, and the novelty instruction predicted compliance better (0.77).

No instruction, knob, push or transplant produced a correct novel algorithm. Iterations 1 to 3 produced 116 correct programs outside the catalogue, and none was new. In iteration 4 none of 14,206 correct programs of the site and subspace experiments was new. In iteration 5, 130 correct programs outside the catalogue, from the base model, the transplant, the panels, the knobs and Gemma, were all labeller misses, known techniques outside the frozen catalogue, catalogue variants or degenerate. The long-form instruction of the request makes most outputs claim novelty while their code is a catalogue algorithm or absent. Within single-sample generation the reachable ideas are the model's known catalogue and, where nudges act, its base model's repertoire; search with an evaluator, which this run did not test, is the documented route to new programs [81, 82].

What remains open follows from this. The first question is what makes a base-diverse task immovable: count primes and valid anagram never moved although the base model writes several correct algorithms for them, while trapped rain water, far less diverse in the base, did. The run has two leads, the runtime merit of the fastest alternative (which tracked movability in panel A but failed on median of two sorted lists) and the early blocks 0 to 11, where a transplant moved kth smallest and connected components more than the main band. A test of either needs the baselines the positioning audit names: weight interpolation between the base and instruct models, layer restoration at the block ranges, and token-level base and aligned collaboration [92, 93, 94, 102]. The second is why the movable set shrank under the bf16 engine and the ban-free unit. The third is whether the base-written intent, the best knob priced so far, holds on more tasks than the five decisive ones. Of the items of the request, the following have been run: the geometry plots in activation coordinates, the top-five prohibition (by prompt, token veto and plan push), natural BFS against DFS internals, thought anchors, the logit and tuned lenses, the same seed test with thinking on and off and with top-k 10, and the mapping of the Jacobian lens and natural language autoencoders. A sparse autoencoder readout that keeps the displacement of the ban was closed rather than delivered.
