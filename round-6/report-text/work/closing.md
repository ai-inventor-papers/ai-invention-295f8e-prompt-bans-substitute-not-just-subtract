# What we have learned so far

After six iterations the run has a firm picture of the collapse, a complete answer on temperature, a confirmed internal handle on the algorithm in Qwen3-8B, a measured map of where three models hold the choice, a measured but incomplete account of what a prohibition does, and no correct novel algorithm. Qwen3-8B [5] writes BFS and Dijkstra almost without exception on the two path search tasks and one algorithm on each of 13 further coding tasks (modal share 1.00 on 12, 0.98 on one). The collapse is not universal: three of 15 new tasks are already mixed in the instruct twin (trapped rain water, default share 0.64; minimum spanning tree weight, 0.66; valid anagram, 0.76), and on two of them the instruct model is more diverse than its base model (twin off-default 0.36 against a base off-default share of 0.23, and 0.34 against 0.11). Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct [22] attempts more algorithms but its correct grid programs are BFS 20 of 20; gemma-3-4b-it [76] writes DFS in 70% and BFS in 30% of programs for one grid prompt; Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct [124] shares most of Qwen3-8B's defaults.

Sampling temperature, top-k, top-p, min-p, thinking mode and random context seeds change how the code is worded, not which algorithm it implements. With the token seed coupled across temperatures from 0.3 to 1.3, the algorithm changed in 0 of 20 seeds on three tasks with thinking off, and in 0 of 20 on the shortest path task with thinking on, under top-p 1 and under top-k 10. Phrasings per idea rose from 3 to 13 clusters on the grid task with thinking off; in the literal cell with thinking on, the clusters rise only at temperature 1.3 (5/7/12 at Jaccard thresholds 0.7/0.8/0.9, against 3/6/10 for a repeat at 0.7). State noise on generated tokens against a temperature matched on per-token KL was not established in bf16 (12:6 and 7:3 discordant flips). On other problems temperature above 1 has been reported to raise algorithmic diversity at a correctness cost [72], so the null belongs to these tasks.

A random displacement of the task-specification states at early to middle blocks, sized to the displacement a ban itself causes, changes the algorithm, and this is a property of Qwen3-8B, not of the 8-bit engine on which it was found. At iteration 4's own absolute displacement, directions and slots, bf16 escapes on 26 of 40 directions on connected components and 6 of 40 on shortest path, against 29 and 8 under FP8 W8A8, with the same directions escaping (Spearman of per-direction shares 0.96 and 0.87), and per-sample plan noise moves 25 and 22 of 50 attempts against 30 and 22. Rounding the activations to e4m3 per token adds nothing; a 5-point per-slot boost on connected components comes from the FP8 weights or the native kernel. The smaller effects that iteration 5 measured in bf16 belong to its ban-free dose unit or its direction seeds: its alpha-2 push was larger at the first edited block than the control's alpha-1 push, which still moved 7 of 50. In bf16 at the ban-free unit, noise at the task-specification positions moves the algorithm (kth smallest 0.37 correct off-default at alpha 2 through blocks 12 to 23; connected components 0.33 at alpha 4 through blocks 0 to 11), while the read position and the condition span barely do; the preregistered site rule was withdrawn for lack of common support and breakage at alpha 8, and a post-hoc model at alpha 4 or less puts the read position below the task-specification site on both tasks. Noise in generated tokens 1 to 32 also redirects the choice. The 8-bit site odds ratio (5.72) and the same-seed contrast (17.25 of 20 seeds) were not rerun in bf16.

Priced as a knob, plan noise is not the practical answer on the evidence so far. Under the 8-bit engine at the ban unit it was the cheapest route to a second correct algorithm on kth smallest (Hill-1 2.72 within 5 pass points) and connected components (2.09); fixed random directions reached above 2 at no cost on kth smallest and two sum, but as maxima over 21 and 9 directions. Against text-level plan samplers, measured in bf16 at the ban-free unit where plan noise was weak, plan noise matched the best sampler on 1 of 5 decisive tasks: an intent sentence written by the base model, with the code written by the instruct model, won on connected components, the shortest path task and two sum (2.53, 1.45 and 1.61), and enumerate and pick reached the most raw diversity at 16 to 62 pass points. That comparison has not been repeated at the absolute unit at which plan noise is strong. Plan noise beat decoding at matched departure on kth smallest at alpha 2 (13:0) and not elsewhere (6:3). Generate then select [43] and enumerate and pick, which hand the choice to an external random draw, remain ceilings with large pass costs.

Where the choice is held has now been measured in three models, and each location fits that model's behaviour. In Qwen3-8B the residual states at the task-specification positions carry a ban's substitute and an instruction's algorithm on the prompt-route tasks: exchanging those states alone into a neutral twin transfers the ban on shortest path (0.80) and kth smallest (0.57) and a forced instruction on shortest path (30 of 30) and connected components (28 of 30), while matched-norm random exchanges and a placebo transfer at most 0.13; on the direct-route tasks the condition span carries the ban. In gemma-3-4b-it the choice between BFS and DFS is made at one generated token after a shared 32-token opening (` visited` leads to DFS with BFS probability 0.011; a blank line, `if` or `queue` lead to BFS at 0.95 to 1.00), about 138 tokens before the code identifies the algorithm. It is readable across prompts from the residual stream at that position (ridge on the raw state R-squared 0.72, a Lasso on Gemma Scope 2 SAE codes 0.66, text 0.18), and DAS finds it in essentially one direction (rank 1 moves the fork log-odds by 8.8 nats); a difference-in-means edit moves it 2.3 times as far as the SAE clamp at matched norm, and every edit toward the minority algorithm breaks programs, so no feature claim is made. In Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct a natural language autoencoder names the algorithm at the token where the prompt names it and not 170 tokens later, and its pre-code explanations name no algorithm; a verbal edit steered two of four tasks only at the generated-token site and large doses, in an exploratory follow-up. In all three models the read position is inert as a carrier. Whether the choice in Qwen3-8B is a low-rank variable, and whether random pushes act through it, is untested: the DAS experiment was lost at planning. The forced-prompt subspace of iteration 4 did not predict which directions move the algorithm (within-task Spearman 0.03), although which directions move is reproducible.

The internal results that hold under bf16 are therefore: the route of a ban (iterations 2 and 3), the band patching on kth smallest and connected components, the exchange of Stage A, the escape fractions and per-sample plan-noise rates of iteration 4 at their absolute unit, the transplant results of iteration 5, the read-position transplant that breaks the output without moving the algorithm, and the post-hoc ordering of noise sites. The 8-bit site odds ratio, the same-seed contrast and the forced-prompt subspace tests are 8-bit results not rerun.

A single prompt ban, where the model obeys it, removes the default and the output collapses on one replacement: Bellman-Ford/SPFA on the shortest path task (272 of 272 compliant iteration-1 programs; 255 of 260 iteration-2 attempts not banned), DFS on the grid task (116 of 128; 257 of 257), and quickselect and size-k heap, brute force, index marking and BFS on the bench tasks where Qwen3-8B obeys (4 of 12 once avoidance counts only catalogue algorithms). Shown a correct program of another algorithm and told not to use it, the model goes home to its default (0.05 of attempts under the copy prompt, 0.72 under the ban), and Llama does the same on five of six present minor bans. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct mostly ignores bans, and its ban of DFS on connected components raised DFS from 29 to 43 correct programs. How often a ban is obeyed depends on its wording more than on anything else measured; the fourth template, which states only that X is not allowed here, is the weakest in every artifact, in Gemma and in Qwen2.5, where all 70 of its programs used the banned default. Bans of five names fail by reimplementation in both families tested: Qwen3-8B and Gemma write a banned algorithm in every program, Gemma moving from its default DFS to its minority BFS (10 to 41 of 50) at half the pass rate, with its prompt states beside a banned centroid in activation space; a push along the top-five direction breaks programs before it moves the algorithm.

What decides the replacement under a modal ban has measured leads and no confirmed rule. The likelihood instrument of iteration 2 cannot decide it, and substitution by the model's stated alternative is not supported in Qwen3-8B. The base model's runner-up names where a push lands in 17 of 21 recorded events across Qwen3-8B and Gemma (5 of 5 on the 8-bit screen, 1 of 2 on the bf16 remeasurement, 3 of 3 and 3 of 4 on new tasks, 5 of 7 in Gemma), and it beats the instruct twin's teacher-forced ranking of algorithm openings 8:1 where the two disagree (p 0.020, retrospective). But where the instruct twin already writes an alternative, that alternative names the landing as often (6 of 7), and on the 8 held-out events where the twin wrote none the base runner-up named 5 (p 0.053): the landing half is descriptive. The base model's own variety does not say whether a task can be moved: over 13 new units its off-default share ranks movability at Spearman 0.15 (95% interval -0.44 to 0.65; power 0.63 at a true 0.6; 0.035 if count inversions' share is taken from its correct programs), so a strong relation is excluded and a moderate one is not; only the runtime merit of the fastest alternative tracks movability after Holm correction (0.71). Both verdicts are conditional on iteration 5's ban-free unit and seeds. Writing the base model's task-specification states into the instruct prefill reopened the base runner-up on 1 of 5 Qwen3-8B tasks and 2 of 7 Gemma tasks, the latter where the instruct twin already wrote it 8% to 12% of the time.

Where a ban acts depends on the task. On the path tasks it is carried mostly by the prompt states (likelihood readout 0.85; behaviour 0.98 on the shortest path task, mixed on the grid task), and Stage A now shows the same for shortest path and kth smallest in behaviour. On the bench, pooled over five gated tasks, the direct route carries 0.744 [0.38, 1.11] of the behavioural effect and the prompt states 0.611 [0.06, 1.17], with overlapping intervals; two sum, classed as direct, also carries 0.43 of its ban in the task-specification states. The ban is enforced at the opening of the program body, to a single token on kth smallest, which is where Gemma's fork falls as well. The read position predicts how strongly a wording will be obeyed (-0.41 across 110 wordings) but carries none of the effect when exchanged or spliced. Push movability as a forecast of which bans a model obeys is closed: inconclusive in the held-out family under its primary rule and disconfirmed under its drop sensitivity.

No instruction, knob, push, transplant, feature clamp or verbal edit produced a correct novel algorithm. Iterations 1 to 3 produced 116 correct programs outside the catalogue and none was new; iteration 4, none of 14,206 correct programs of the site and subspace experiments; iteration 5, none of 130 correct programs outside the catalogue. In iteration 6 a second blind adjudication of the 14 transplant programs that two of three judges had called novel, by two model families the run had not used and with ten controls of known class, found 1 novel call by one judge (a linear search, overturned on a full read) and 0 by the other, a final count of 0; the exchange experiment, the Gemma experiment (1 of 1,450 correct programs outside the catalogue) and the NLA experiment (10 correct programs labelled other) found none either. The long-form instruction of the request makes most outputs claim novelty while their code is a catalogue algorithm or absent. Within single-sample generation the reachable ideas are the model's known catalogue; search with an evaluator, which this run did not test, is the documented route to new programs [81, 82].

What remains open follows from this. The first question is whether the algorithm choice in Qwen3-8B is a low-rank variable at the task-specification positions: DAS fitted against the Stage A exchanges, and the projection of iteration 4's 40 directions onto the fitted subspace, would test it, and the engine control has made those directions a stable target. The second is what makes the ban-free unit weaker than the ban unit at a larger scalar dose: its per-position and per-block profile or its direction seeds. The third is whether a small push at Gemma's fork switches the algorithm without breaking the program. The fourth is whether base intent, the best knob priced so far, holds on more tasks and against plan noise at its absolute unit. Of the items of the request, the following have been run: the geometry plots in activation coordinates (at a label-dependent position in iteration 2, at the plan site in iteration 4, and for the top-five prohibition in Qwen3-8B and Gemma in iteration 6), the top-five prohibition by prompt, token veto and plan push, natural BFS against DFS internals (within a ban in Qwen3-8B, within one prompt in Gemma), thought anchors, the logit and tuned lenses, the same seed test with thinking on and off and with top-k 10, natural language autoencoders (on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), sparse autoencoders (Qwen-Scope on Qwen3-8B, Gemma Scope 2 on Gemma) and a first-order attribution in place of a circuit graph. The manifold of algorithms in a causally fitted plan subspace was planned and not run.
